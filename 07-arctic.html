The Frigid Sea at the Edge of the World

We are now in the polar region, home to the smallest and least-explored of Earth’s oceans — the Arctic Ocean. Spanning vast expanses around the North Pole, it has severely restricted water exchange with neighbouring ocean basins. Bordered by the coasts of Eurasia and North America and fed by major Siberian rivers, the Arctic Ocean is noticeably less salty than any other ocean.

Historically known as the Frigid Sea, in the depths of winter nearly its entire surface is covered in sea ice, which winds and currents buckle into jagged pressure ridges. Beneath the ice, mysterious phenomena unfold, such as internal waves that can tower tens of meters high even while the ocean surface remains glassy calm. Meanwhile, the Atlantic and Pacific supply their northern neighbour with heat — meaning that, especially in winter, the Arctic Ocean does not chill the surrounding landmasses, but actually warms vast stretches of land.

Although the central polar basin remains accessible only to powerful nuclear-powered icebreakers, the region’s strategic importance for maritime transport is steadily growing along the Northern Sea Route.

Harsh polar conditions complicate mineral exploration, yet significant deposits of oil, natural gas, metallic ores, and ferromanganese nodules have already been discovered — alongside construction sands, gravel deposits, and placer gold. Several Arctic seas also serve as traditional grounds for commercial fishing, marine mammal hunting, and kelp harvesting.

  • The Arctic Ocean serves as one of the climate system’s primary ‘refrigerators’
  • It contains the widest continental shelf seas in the world
  • In some parts of the ocean, sea ice reaches up to 5 metres in thickness
  • Murmansk is its only major ice-free port
  • It shares Greenland — the world’s largest island — with the Atlantic Ocean
  • It is known for ‘dead water’, a hazardous maritime phenomenon where a layer of freshwater sits atop denser seawater, creating drag that slows vessels down
  • The White Sea gets its name from being covered in ice and snow for roughly 200 days a year
  • If all the glaciers in the Arctic region were to melt, global sea levels would rise by roughly 10 metres

世界尽头的寒冰之海

我们正位于极地地区，这里坐落着最小、最不为人知的大洋——北冰洋。它环绕北极点延展，与其他大洋的水体连通通道十分有限。欧亚大陆与北美洲的海岸将它环抱；西伯利亚多条大河注入海域，因此北冰洋盐度低于其他大洋。

古人称它为寒冰之海。隆冬时节，洋面几乎全部被冰层覆盖，冰层受风力与洋流挤压形成冰脊。冰脊遮蔽了研究者的视线，掩盖住北冰洋整片水体。这片海域会发生诸多奇妙现象，例如内波。内波生成于淡水与咸水的交界面，波高可达数十米，而海面却风平浪静。大西洋与太平洋为这位“兄弟大洋”输送热量，因此北冰洋，尤其在冬季，非但不会冷却陆地，反而会为广袤的大陆带来显著增温效应。

极地中央盆地只有大功率核动力破冰船才能抵达，但这片水域的航运价值持续提升，货物运输主要依靠北海航线开展。

严酷的自然条件给矿产勘探带来阻碍，但这里已经探明石油、天然气、各类金属矿、铁锰结核、建筑用砂与砾石矿，还发现了砂金矿。部分海域是传统渔业、海洋哺乳动物捕猎以及海藻开采作业区。

  • 气候系统的“冷却器”之一
  • 全球面积最宽的大陆架浅海
  • 大洋某些区域的冰层厚度可达5米
  • 唯一的大型不冻港——摩尔曼斯克
  • 与大西洋“共享”世界最大岛屿——格陵兰岛
  • 发现对船舶构成巨大威胁的“死水现象”：咸水层之间漂浮淡水层
  • 白海之所以得名，是因为一年中有200天被冰覆盖
  • 倘若大洋全部冰川消融，世界大洋海平面将上升10米
