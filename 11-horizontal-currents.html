Horizontal Currents: The Invisible Rivers of the Ocean

Wind patterns are the primary driver behind the movement of massive volumes of ocean water. When winds blow persistently in one direction, friction builds up at the sea surface, setting the upper layers of the ocean in motion. For example, the trade winds generate currents that flow from east to west. Between the trade-wind belts lies a nearly windless region known as the equatorial doldrums. During the age of sail, this zone earned a grim reputation: ships would sit becalmed for weeks awaiting a breeze, freshwater supplies would dwindle, and mutinies frequently broke out. On either side of the equator, currents form vast circular systems called ocean gyres. The Atlantic, Pacific, and Indian Oceans each contain two major gyres — one in the Northern Hemisphere and one in the Southern Hemisphere — driven primarily by the Earth’s rotation and the Coriolis effect. While surface currents in the open ocean generally move slowly, coastal currents — especially when combined with tides — can reach speeds dangerous to swimmers and small vessels adrift.

Gaspard-Gustave de Coriolis (1792–1843)

French mathematician, physicist, and mechanical engineer. He demonstrated that the Earth acts as a rotating frame of reference in which any moving object experiences an apparent acceleration. This gives rise to an inertial force that deflects moving objects to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere — the Coriolis force.

What Happened to the Plastic Ducks?

In 1992, a shipping container holding roughly 28,000 plastic bath toys washed overboard in the North Pacific. Over the next 20 years this flotilla drifted with the currents. Most settled into the Great Pacific Garbage Patch. These items became ocean tracers — tools used to study currents.

What Lies Beneath the Surface?

For a long time, scientists believed that ocean layers lying more than a few hundred metres deep — out of direct contact with the atmosphere — were largely sluggish and flowed in the same direction as surface currents. In the second half of the 20th century, this understanding of deep-water dynamics changed dramatically with the discovery of large-scale subsurface and deep countercurrents. These currents flow opposite to prevailing regional winds or against the direction of major persistent surface currents. In both scale and water transport volume, they generally surpass surface flows. For example, the Pacific Equatorial Undercurrent stretches roughly 15,000 km and carries a greater volume of water than the Gulf Stream. Although direct instrumental measurements of subsurface and deep currents remain relatively scarce, they are sufficient to prove that water circulates continuously from the surface down to the deepest ocean trenches.

In the Atlantic Ocean, the subsurface Lomonosov Current was discovered flowing beneath the South Equatorial Current; in the Pacific Ocean, the Cromwell Current was found beneath the Kuroshio and Equatorial Currents; and in the Indian Ocean, the Tareev Current was identified.

水平运动，（或）海洋中看不见的河流

风向是驱动巨量海水移动的主要原因。当气流长期保持固定方向，会在海洋表面产生摩擦力，带动表层海水流动。例如信风作用下会形成自东向西的洋流。信风带之间，存在一片几乎无风的区域 —— 赤道无风带。在帆船时代，这里臭名昭著：船只可以一连数星期停滞原地等待风起，淡水储备消耗殆尽，船上还时常爆发哗变。赤道两侧的洋流形成环形环流，即大洋环流。大西洋、太平洋、印度洋各拥有两组环流，南北半球各一组。环流形成的主要诱因是地球自转与科里奥利力。开阔大洋表层洋流流速并不算大；但近岸洋流，尤其叠加潮汐作用时，流速足以对游泳者和失控的小型船只构成危险。

古斯塔夫·加斯帕尔·科里奥利（1792–1843）

法国数学家、力学家、工程师。证明地球属于转动参考系，运动物体会获得附加加速度。由此产生的惯性力，使北半球运动物体向右偏转，南半球向左偏转，这就是科里奥利力。

塑料小黄鸭遭遇了什么？

北太平洋一艘货轮失事，一个装有约28,000件塑料玩具的集装箱坠入海中。此后二十年间，这支“玩具大军”跟随洋流四处漂泊。大多数玩具定居在太平洋大垃圾带。这些物品不由自主地成为了示踪器——研究洋流的工具。

深海之下藏着什么？

长久以来人们认为，数百米以下不与大气直接接触的深层水体几乎静止，流向与表层海水一致。二十世纪下半叶，人类对深层水动力学的认知彻底改写：科学家发现次表层与深层大规模逆流。它们流向与该区域盛行风向、主流稳定洋流相反。其规模和流量通常超过表层洋流。例如，太平洋的次表层赤道逆流长度约15,000公里，流量超过墨西哥湾流。虽然次表层、深层洋流实测数据尚不充分，但已足够证明：从海面直至大洋最深处，海水始终处于持续运动状态。

在大西洋南赤道暖流之下，发现了次表层赤道逆流——罗蒙诺索夫洋流；在太平洋黑潮和赤道流之下——克伦威尔洋流；在印度洋——塔列耶夫洋流。
