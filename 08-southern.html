The Southern Ocean and the White Continent

At the opposite end of the world from the Arctic Ocean lies the Southern Ocean — the polar opposite of its northern counterpart. Completely open to the global ocean system, it connects with all other oceans through the Antarctic Circumpolar Current — the most powerful and longest current on Earth (~28,000 km), crossing every meridian on the globe. Here, continental shelves plunge far deeper than anywhere else, colossal icebergs drift, and winds sweep unimpeded across vast distances.

The Southern Ocean shoulders the immense burden of absorbing excess planetary heat and atmospheric carbon dioxide. It encircles and guards Antarctica — Earth’s largest glaciated region and highest continent (averaging 2,330 m above sea level), whose ice sheet locks away roughly 70% of the planet’s freshwater. Here, one of the greatest discoveries of the 20th century was made: subglacial Lake Vostok. Though inhospitable at first glance, the waters beneath the surface conceal a universe teeming with life — home to nearly ten thousand species, many found nowhere else on Earth.

Together, the Southern Ocean and the White Continent form a giant natural laboratory. Yet despite decades of scientific research, this region remains largely pristine — earning it the name the Last Ocean.

  • The Southern Ocean is home to the Weddell Sea, the clearest sea in the world, with water visibility reaching 79 metres
  • Over 200,000 icebergs drift in its waters at any given time, some of them ‘veterans’ lasting 12 to 15 years
  • It borders the world’s largest ice shelves — the Ronne Ice Shelf (300,000 km²) and the Ross Ice Shelf (487,000 km²)
  • Near Cape Burks on the coast of Marie Byrd Land lies the ‘Pole of Winds’, where gusts reach 75 m/s (270 km/h)
  • Antarctica is the driest and coldest continent on Earth — recording a low of −89.2 °C in 1983 — and has the highest surface reflectivity (albedo) at 99%
  • Antarctica belongs to no single nation and is a demilitarized zone where commercial mineral extraction is prohibited, though scientific geological exploration is permitted
  • At the Amundsen–Scott South Pole Station, you can walk across all 24 time zones in just a few seconds
  • Antarctica hosts an annual ice marathon — a 100-kilometre ski race

南大洋与白色大陆

在距离北冰洋的地球另一端，延伸着南大洋——它是其极地兄弟的对立面。南大洋完全接入全球大洋系统，依靠全球势力最强、流程最长（约28,000千米）、横贯地球全部经线的南极绕极流，和其余各大洋彼此连通。这里大陆架深度要大得多，漂浮着地球上体积最大的冰山，海域遍布涡旋；气团可以长距离移动，全程不会受到任何陆地阻挡。

南大洋承担着重大使命，吸收地球多余热量以及大气当中的二氧化碳。它守护着地球最大冰川区、最高大陆（海拔2330米）——南极洲，其冰盖中封存着地球约70%的淡水。20世纪重大科学发现之一便诞生于此：沃斯托克冰下湖。乍看之下这里环境严酷，但海面之下，却是生机盎然的世界。近一万种生物在此栖息，其中大量物种为当地独有。

南大洋与白色大陆是一座巨型实验室，人类在此探索地球自然奥秘。尽管已经开展多年科考，这片区域依旧近乎保持原始状态，故而也被称作最后的大洋。

  • 世界上最干净的海，水体透明度达79米——威德尔海
  • 同时存在超过20万座冰山，其中部分是年龄达12–15年的“老居民”
  • 世界上最大的冰架——龙尼冰架（30万平方千米）和罗斯冰架（48.7万平方千米）
  • “风极”——玛丽·伯德地海岸伯克斯角附近，风速可达75米/秒或270公里/小时
  • 南极洲是最干燥、最寒冷的大陆：-89.2°C（1983年），反照率最高——达99%
  • 南极洲不属于任何国家，是非军事化区域，禁止开采矿产, 但允许勘探
  • 在阿蒙森-斯科特站，可以在几秒钟内跨越24个时区
  • 南极洲每年举办冰上马拉松——100公里滑雪赛
