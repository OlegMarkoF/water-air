What is Pollution?

Pollution is anything in the wrong place, at the wrong time, and in the wrong amount. Environmental contamination can occur naturally — through events like floods or volcanic eruptions — or as a result of human activity.

Pollutants are chemical compounds whose concentrations exceed critical thresholds, entering the environment faster than an ecosystem can transform or assimilate them.

When assessing pollution levels, scientists rely on the concept of maximum permissible concentration (MPC) — the highest concentration of a substance in the environment that, under continuous or temporary exposure, does not disrupt natural processes or significantly harm ecosystem components and natural communities as a whole.

Ocean Pollution

The primary pollutants entering the World Ocean are oil, plastics, heavy metals, pesticides, and untreated wastewater. The largest share of marine pollution originates from land-based river runoff. Atmospheric transport and deposition rank second, while localized sources — such as municipal sewage, ocean dumping, and industrial waste runoff — round out the top three. Once in the ocean, pollutants are distributed unevenly, concentrating largely in coastal zones and the upper surface layer. Ocean mixing, along with horizontal currents and vertical circulation, transports these contaminants across vast distances and down into the deep ocean. There, bound to suspended particulate matter, they settle onto the seabed, accumulate, and can become a source of secondary pollution.

Atmospheric Pollution

The primary atmospheric pollutants are greenhouse gases, sulphur dioxide, particulate matter, and smog. They originate from extractive industries, the energy sector, agriculture, and transportation. Air pollution is particularly hazardous because it is highly mobile and can affect virtually every component of the environment across vast areas. It disrupts photosynthesis, transpiration, and respiration, degrades soil health, and contributes to ocean pollution, while also triggering respiratory diseases, allergies, and other health conditions. To standardize atmospheric monitoring, scientists introduced the Air Quality Index (AQI). In 2016, 91% of the world’s population lived in areas where air pollution exceeded safe guideline limits.

Physical Pollution

Physical pollution involves alterations to the physical parameters of the ocean and atmosphere. It manifests as shifts in temperature, noise levels, light intensity, background radiation, vibration, and electromagnetic fields, all of which can harm individual organisms and entire ecosystems.

Noise Pollution

Living organisms have evolved to adapt to natural ambient sounds, whereas anthropogenic noise introduces novel, disruptive stimuli into the environment. The primary sources of human-generated noise are transportation — including cars, trains, ships, and aircraft — along with construction and infrastructure development, such as building roads and laying pipelines and cables.

Radioactive Contamination

Naturally occurring radioisotopes continuously enter and cycle out of the environment through natural processes. However, these natural cycles are overwhelmed by the influx of synthetic, anthropogenic radioisotopes. Radioactive contamination of the atmosphere is far more devastating than waterborne contamination because air masses circulate much faster than ocean currents.

Thermal Pollution

Thermal pollution occurs when ambient environmental temperatures rise above natural levels due to the discharge of heated wastewater, industrial exhaust gases, and greenhouse gas emissions. In aquatic ecosystems, this warming triggers eutrophication and alters species composition.

Electromagnetic Pollution

Electromagnetic pollution is generated by electrical installations, power transmission lines, and radio transmitters. It is primarily an atmospheric form of pollution, as submarine communications cables are encased in multi-layered insulation with protective shielding.

Light Pollution

Light pollution occurs when artificial light brightens the night sky — a phenomenon known as skyglow — as light scatters through the lower atmosphere. This disrupts the circadian rhythms of living organisms, sometimes proving fatal. However, this type of pollution has minimal impact on the open ocean.

Chemical Pollution

Chemical pollution occurs when substances that are quantitatively or qualitatively foreign to an ecosystem — such as heavy metals — enter the environment. While nature has evolved finely tuned mechanisms to neutralize some of these substances, human activity introduces synthetic compounds that never existed naturally, leaving ecosystems with no biological pathways to break them down.

Biological Pollution

Biological pollution involves contamination by biological agents and their metabolic byproducts. This includes the accidental introduction of non-native plants and animals, as well as the spread of viruses, parasites, and pathogens through wastewater and atmospheric currents.

什么是污染？

污染，就是物质出现在错误的地点、错误的时间，并且超出合理的数量。环境污染既可以由自然过程造成（洪水、火山喷发等），也可以源于人类活动。

污染物，即各类化学化合物，其浓度超过临界标准，流入环境的速度超过生态系统自身转化、消解吸收的速度。

评估污染时会用到最大容许浓度这一概念：即环境中某物质的最大含量，在持续或短暂接触条件下，不会破坏自然进程，几乎不对生态系统组分以及整个生物群落造成影响。

海洋污染

世界海洋的主要污染物：石油、塑料、重金属、杀虫剂、未经处理的生活污水。绝大部分污染物经由陆地河流汇入海洋；其次是大气沉降；第三位是本地污染源：生活污水排放、废弃物倾倒、工业废料冲刷入海等。进入世界大洋的污染物分布并不均匀，大部分集中在近海海域与海水上层。大洋水体的混合、水平与垂直洋流，会把污染物输送至遥远海域和海洋深层；污染物以悬浮物形式沉降至海底并不断累积，进而可以成为二次污染的源头。

大气污染

大气主要污染物包括温室气体、二氧化硫、粉尘、光化学烟雾。污染源来自采掘工业、能源行业、农业以及交通运输。大气污染具备流动性，会在广大范围内几乎影响环境全部组成要素。它破坏光合作用、蒸腾作用、呼吸作用，恶化土壤状态，造成海洋污染，还会诱发呼吸系统疾病、过敏症等。为了系统化关于大气状况的知识，引入了空气质量指数的概念。2016年，全球91%人口居住在空气污染超标的区域。

物理性污染

源于海洋、大气各项物理参数发生改变。表现为温度、噪声水平、光照、本底辐射、振动、电磁场以及其他参数的变化，会危害单个生物乃至整个生态系统。

噪声（声音）污染

自然界存在生物演化已经适应的天然噪声；同时也存在人为噪声，这类噪声对于环境而言属于外来新生干扰。人为噪声主要来源：汽车、火车、船舶、飞机等交通工具，还有建筑施工、管线与电缆铺设工程等。

放射性污染

天然放射性同位素不断进入环境，并通过自然循环排出，但自然循环无法应对人为来源的额外放射性同位素进入。大气放射性污染危害更大，原因是大气气团的移动速度远高于水体。

热污染

于废水排放、高温废气与温室气体排放，造成环境温度高于自然本底水平。在水体中会引发水体富营养化，改变生物物种组成。

电磁污染

其原因是电气装置、输电线路和无线电发射设备的运行。主要属于大气类污染；海底通信电缆具备多层屏蔽绝缘层，受此影响较小。

光污染

人造光源造成夜空泛光，光线在低层大气散射，扰乱生物节律，甚至造成生物死亡。该类污染对海洋影响较小。

化学污染

向生态系统输入本身不存在，或是数量异常的物质（例如重金属）。大自然拥有部分天然消解机制，但人类向环境排放很多自然界原本不存在的化合物，没有天然途径可以将其中和。

生物性污染

由各类生物媒介以及它们代谢产物带来的污染。包括动植物意外入侵扩散，病毒、寄生虫、致病微生物随污水、大气环流传播扩散。
